# Avara
---
# **A Continent of Many Powers**

Avara is a vast continent of rival kingdoms, distinct races, and unfinished disputes. Race and blood remain among the deepest wedges dividing one person from another. Never in its history has the continent known a single unified realm. Many have tried to create one, **all have failed.**

Human kingdoms, elven states, dwarven holds, and orc tribes have risen across the land only to fall in turn. Borders shift constantly. Old rulers are cast down, and new ones rise to inherit the same ambitions left behind by those who came before them. Five generations may be long enough for a kingdom to disappear, yet it is never enough for transgression to be forgiven.

Thousands of thrones harbor ambitions to rule the land. Treaties may quiet a border for a time, but they cannot erase the past. Every race remembers what was taken from them, and grief has always found a way to linger in the minds of the living long after those who first suffered it are gone.

Every ruler inherits a map covered in unfinished ambitions. Each believes they stand on the right side of history. Many claim they can hear the land itself yearning to be united beneath a single banner. At night, when they finally close their eyes, they dream of glory, prestige, conquest, and the immortality promised to those whose names survive long after their bodies have returned to the earth.

Yet beneath those glorious dreams lies something else.

Fear.

It follows those who wear the crowns quietly, patiently, and persistently. Across generations, it has whispered the same warning into the minds of rulers: **If you do not draw first blood, then you will be the one who bleeds.**

And so millions of soldiers march across Avara.

They march beneath banners bearing the names of rulers and Gods. One soldier kills for glory and pride, while beside him another drags a civilian by the hair, seeking to satisfy an anger inherited from his long dead ancestors. Others kill in the names of their Gods, offering lives as tribute to the divine. Some march for land, others for vengeance, and many simply because someone above them gave the order.

In the end, the reason matters little to those caught beneath it. Whatever banners are raised and whatever claims of legitimacy are spoken, it is the innocent who are so often made to pay the price.

The fate of the conquered rests entirely upon the whims of the victorious. Across Avara, countless people have questioned the heavens as they drew their final breath, searching for some meaning behind the fate that had befallen them. Those who survive may discover that survival offers little mercy of its own. Throughout the continent can be found eyes that have lost the flicker of hope, belonging to men and women forced to walk in chains while someone behind them carries a whip.

**This is Avara.**

A continent of poeples united beneath their own banners and divided against everyone who stands beyond them. Yet when every hope of lasting peace seems spent, one Empire looks upon this endless division and sees something greater than ambition.

It sees **a responsibility owed to the land**.

Avara must be united beneath one law, one crown, and a single throne standing above all others. So the armies of the Empire march, and the kingdoms in their path prepare for the invasion. Walls are strengthened. Armies are assembled. Roads are watched for the first sight of Imperial banners.

When those banners finally appear, many defenders are confronted by something they did not expect.

Imperial standards are carried upright by soldiers trained for war since their teenage years. The sound of the approaching army reaches the walls before individual faces become clear. Thousands of boots striking the road with an almost unnatural unity. Humans, elves, dwarves, halflings, Goliaths, Half Orcs, and Half Elves march beside one another beneath the same banners, preparing to conquer kingdoms whose rulers once believed such unity impossible.

To many rulers beyond its borders, the Empire is an abomination, a manifestation of everything wrong with the world. Its machines challenge what others once believed to be the limits of nature. Its scholars take whatever knowledge proves useful, improve it where they can, and eventually speak of it as part of Imperial civilization. Traditions that endured for thousands of years are erased the moment the Empire decides they have no place in the future it intends to build.

The Empire has looked upon the vast land stretching before it. The continent has been measured, and it has been weighed. To bring Avara beneath a single order and guide it toward its first true age of peace is a responsibility the Empire believes only it alone can bear. 

Many call it arrogance.

**The Empire calls it destiny.**
