# **Halflings**

<img class="race-banner" src="../../assets/images/races/halfling.png" alt="Halflings of Avara">

## **Overview**

Halflings are often considered the strangest civilization in Avara.

While other peoples compete for territory, wealth, influence, and power, halflings are more likely to spend their time deciding what they will have for dinner. There is no known halfling civilization larger than a small city. Unlike many other races, halflings have little interest in building great kingdoms or expanding their borders.

Instead, they live in scattered villages, farming communities, and merchant towns.

Halflings have a strong sense of when something is enough. A warm meal, a comfortable home, good company, and honest work are enough to make many of them content. Other races sometimes mistake this attitude for laziness, but halflings do believe in hard work.

They simply believe that enjoying the present is just as important as preparing for the future.

## **The Halfling Body**

Halflings are small humanoids, usually standing around three feet tall. Their bodies are compact, but their proportions remain recognizably adult despite their height.

Halflings age very slowly in appearance, although their actual lifespans are similar to those of humans. Because of this, judging a halfling's age by appearance alone is extremely difficult. A middle-aged halfling may look remarkably young, sometimes resembling someone who has not yet reached adulthood.

Halflings are also naturally nimble and dexterous. They can climb, crawl, squeeze, and reach places that larger people would struggle to enter.

Combined with their youthful appearance and easygoing behavior, a group of adult halflings working in a field may easily be mistaken for children playing among the crops.

## **Uncanny Luck**

Halflings are absurdly lucky.

No scholar has found a convincing explanation for how or why this happens, but there is no shortage of examples. A halfling might accidentally wander into the path of a stampede, only to be found afterward without so much as a bruise. An arrow aimed directly at a halfling's head might miss because, at that exact moment, the halfling crouched down to pick up a silver coin lying in the dirt.

Halflings themselves rarely consider these events unusual. Ask one how they survived something that should have killed them, and the answer is often little more than:

“I don't know.”

Then they go home and start preparing dinner.

## **Tribes and Communities**

Halflings have historically lived in small, loosely organized communities rather than powerful states. Their villages and small cities usually govern themselves according to whatever arrangement keeps daily life running smoothly, arguments reasonably short, and dinner more or less on time.

Leadership is practical rather than ceremonial. Someone trusted by the community may speak on behalf of everyone else, but formal institutions usually appear only when they become necessary.

This has occasionally caused problems for foreign diplomats. A diplomat might arrive in a halfling settlement and ask to speak with its ruler. The halflings will usually discuss the matter among themselves for a while before returning with another question:

“What is a ruler?”

## **Fear and Violence**

The exact rate of mana attunement among halflings is unknown, though the closest estimate is around one in ten. The lack of reliable information has a simple explanation: many halfling Praedari do not live like Praedari at all.

A halfling capable of becoming a powerful warrior or mage may instead choose to spend their life farming, trading, raising a family, or practicing some ordinary craft. For them, a productive day followed by a warm meal at home may be more appealing than fame or power.

This easygoing nature sometimes causes outsiders to believe halflings are easy to exploit. Few assumptions about halflings are more dangerous.

When faced with danger, halflings can become remarkably calm and calculating. Their bravery is different from the reckless charge of an orc or the stubborn willingness of a goliath to fight for honor until death.

Halfling courage is practical, they observe the situation, measure the risks, consider their chances, and act accordingly. Their small size already makes them difficult targets. Their natural agility makes them harder to catch. Add their uncanny luck, and dealing with a determined group of halflings can become a miserable experience very quickly.

Halfling settlements may be small, they may appear poorly defended, they may even look harmless. Those who try to conquer one usually discover that the trouble involved is far greater than anything they expected to gain.

## **The Vassals**

Many great kingdoms prefer to deal with halfling communities through carefully negotiated agreements involving taxes, levies, protection, and vassalage. In exchange for cooperation, halfling settlements often receive military protection, roads, infrastructure, and access to the wider economy. As a result, many halfling villages and cities exist under the patronage of larger kingdoms while continuing to manage most of their own affairs.

Humans, elves, and dwarves tend to agree on one thing when dealing with halflings:

The best way to profit from them is through a clear agreement of give and take. Halflings are known for taking agreements seriously. They will not give more than what was promised, but they will not give less either.

If a halfling village agrees to pay a certain amount of tax, the payment will arrive when it is supposed to, down to the last coin. For this reason, halfling communities are often considered reliable investments by the kingdoms that protect them. They will rarely provide endless wealth or fuel great ambitions. But they are stable and when the tax collector arrives, the gold will be there.
