    Elves¶

    The Long View¶

    An elf may watch a human family grow, age, and disappear while the elf is still in the strength of adulthood.

    Elves mature at roughly the same pace as humans, then age more slowly. Without significant mana cultivation, a healthy elf may live around one hundred and seventy years.

    That long life changes the scale of things. A decision made today may still shape an elf’s world a century later. Patience comes more easily when there is time to wait and when the consequences may arrive within your own lifetime.

    The Elven Body¶

    Pointed ears are the clearest sign, though an elf’s sharper hearing, keener eyesight, and finely shaped features may give them away first. Many people consider elves unusually beautiful. Elves themselves are rarely unaware of their advantages.

    Many have noticed that Elves themselves as races have a low libido compared to humans. This affects their low birth rate, and slow population growth.

    Mana, Learned Over Generations¶

    Mana is the elves’ greatest natural advantage.

    About three in ten elves become Praedari. Many sense, control, and cultivate mana more readily than people of other ancestries. But an elven Praedari is not automatically stronger than a Praedari of another people. Talent still has to be trained.

    The deeper advantage is older than any one practitioner.

    For thousands of years, elven scholars have studied mana: how to cultivate it, shape it into spells, bind it into objects, and use it in healing, artificing, and enchantment. Each generation inherits the work of those before it.

    An elf may not begin with greater power. They may begin with more knowledge already waiting to be learned.

    Few, and Slowly Replaced¶

    Elven female fertility is extremely low. Couples may spend years hoping for a child, and purely elven populations grow slowly.

    That makes war costly in a way few victories can hide. A ruined field may be replanted. A broken building may be raised again. Thousands of elves lost in battle may take generations to replace.

    Elven caution is not always fear. Sometimes it is the memory of what a triumph can cost the grandchildren.

    Pride and Its Many Faces¶

    Elves live longer, perceive more, and often have an easier path to mana. Their pride grew among real advantages and real achievements.

    Some wear it as quiet confidence. Others make sure the room knows exactly where they believe they stand.

    Pride can drive an elf toward mastery. It can also make failure hard to bear, especially when it comes in a field where they were raised to believe elves excel.

    There is no single elven way of being proud. High Elves, Wood Elves, and Dark Elves have each given that pride a different shape.

    Elven pride has built wonders. It has also taught more than one elf to mistake an advantage for proof of superiority.
