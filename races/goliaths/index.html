    Goliaths¶

    The Mountain Blood¶

    Goliaths originated in the mountains, and much of that ancestry remains visible even among families that have lived elsewhere for generations. Roughly one in five Goliaths is mana-attuned. Their bodies are naturally suited to cold weather, thin air, steep terrain, and long climbs, allowing them to endure conditions that would exhaust most other peoples. Centuries of migration have changed this somewhat, however, and not every modern Goliath is equally comfortable in the mountains.

    Without significant mana cultivation, a Goliath can live for around two hundred and twenty years. They reproduce more slowly than humans, though still faster than elves, and commonly stand around seven feet tall, towering over most other peoples of Avara.

    Despite their long lives and physical strength, Goliaths are among the rarest peoples on the continent. Their scarcity has less to do with low numbers than with how widely those numbers are spread. Goliath families rarely remain concentrated in one place for many generations. A family may settle somewhere for decades, perhaps even longer, but eventually sons and daughters begin leaving to find their own paths.

    Goliaths can be found almost anywhere in Avara, but rarely in great numbers.

    The Wandering People¶

    There is no sacred pilgrimage that sends Goliaths into the world, nor any traditional age at which they are expected to leave home. Their reasons for wandering can be important or completely trivial. Beneath them all, however, lies the same old curiosity about what might exist beyond the horizon.

    A hunter may cross an ocean after hearing stories of a beast they have never seen. A craftsman may travel for years simply to study a foreign technique. Another Goliath may leave because someone once described a distant city badly enough that they decide they must see it for themselves. Their wandering is rarely aimless to them, curiosity is reason enough.

    Traditional Goliath communities are usually gatherings of families rather than formal tribes. Related families, close friends, and old companions may remain together for generations, but permanent chiefs and hereditary rulers are uncommon. Leadership tends to exist only when circumstances require it, and authority rarely survives simply because someone's parents once possessed it.

    Many disputes in traditional Goliath society have ultimately been resolved in a very Goliath fashion: one person decides there is a large world beyond the argument and leaves to see more of it.

    Honor and Competition¶

    Goliath culture places enormous importance on honor, honesty, and demonstrated ability. A promise given should be kept, and a fair defeat should be accepted without excuses. Claiming expertise while refusing to prove it brings embarrassment, while cheating in a contest whose rules were agreed upon beforehand is considered deeply dishonorable.

    Competition is therefore common among Goliaths. Craftsmanship, medicine, music, storytelling, hunting, navigation, cooking, and countless other pursuits can become fields in which a Goliath seeks mastery.

    What matters is not what someone is good at, but whether they have truly earned the right to call themselves good at it.

    A physically weak Goliath who becomes a brilliant physician may command far greater respect than a powerful warrior who has accomplished nothing beyond being born strong.

    Tattoos and Reputation¶

    Traditional Goliath tattoos often record important achievements from the bearer's life: great hunts, mastercrafted works, journeys completed, contests won, or other deeds the individual considers worthy of remembrance. On an older Goliath, the body can become something close to a biography written across the skin.

    To outsiders, these tattoos may appear to be little more than elaborate patterns and unfamiliar symbols. To a Goliath who understands them, they can tell the story of a person's life: what they attempted, what they accomplished, and what they believed was worth carrying with them forever.

    Because of this, falsely claiming an achievement through a tattoo is considered an especially serious disgrace. Such a mark is not merely dishonest decoration. It is a lie deliberately carved into the body and displayed openly before anyone who knows how to read it.

    For a Goliath who chooses to carry such a lie, the punishment is death.

    Language and Faith¶

    Centuries of migration have produced many Goliath dialects, sometimes different enough that two distant communities may initially struggle to understand one another. All of them, however, descend from the same older tongue, and common roots remain recognizable. Given enough time together, Goliaths from distant parts of Avara can usually become accustomed to one another's speech.

    This shared ancestry shapes how they often regard unfamiliar Goliaths. A stranger from the other side of the continent may still be treated much like a distant cousin whose family simply took a different road centuries ago.

    Traditional Goliath culture is not strongly religious. Individual Goliaths may become deeply faithful, especially those who settle among other peoples, but historically their communities have placed little authority in temples, priesthoods, or religious institutions. Faith is neither forbidden nor mocked. It simply occupies a less central place than it does in many other cultures.

    Their attention tends to settle more naturally upon things that can be experienced, tested, accomplished, or proven.

    Goliaths and Other Peoples¶

    Goliaths frequently live among other peoples and often become part of foreign settlements, armies, workshops, merchant communities, or families of close companions. Romantic relationships outside their own people nevertheless remain uncommon, and no reliably documented mixed Goliath offspring are known.

    Most scholars therefore believe that Goliaths cannot reproduce with other peoples, though the evidence remains limited. With Goliaths scattered so widely across Avara and their numbers already small, certainty has proven difficult to obtain.

    Goliath slaves are similarly rare. A Goliath may understand defeat and accept that losing has consequences. They may endure imprisonment, punishment, ransom, or even voluntary service to someone who defeated them fairly. What they generally reject is the belief that defeat gives another person permanent ownership over their body, choices, and future.

    For many Goliaths, bondage crosses a line that defeat does not. Many have chosen death rather than permanent slavery.

    The Measure of a Goliath¶

    Goliaths possess no shared empire, king, homeland, or single unified language. Centuries of wandering have scattered them across Avara until two Goliaths born thousands of miles apart may share little beyond distant ancestry and a handful of recognizable customs.

    Yet certain values survived the distance.

    A Goliath is expected to stand behind what they claim. A promise should be kept. A defeat honestly earned should be accepted. Achievement must be proven rather than declared, and reputation should be built from deeds rather than inheritance.

    Life is too large to spend entirely in one place and when it puts something before you that is truly worthy of your attention, do not hesitate.

    Run towards it.
