    Dark Elves¶

    Overview¶

    Among the major elven cultures, Dark Elves are the most openly violent, ambitious, and cruel. They take pride in strength, results, and the ability to survive in a society where weakness can be fatal.

    High Elves turn elven pride toward perfection. Wood Elves temper it through balance. Dark Elves embody pride without much apology, and they make sure others know it.

    Their reputation is rarely an innocent misunderstanding. Stories told about Dark Elves are often softened by the time they reach other peoples.

    The Lands They Chose

    Dark Elves built their oldest kingdoms in cold coasts, mountains, black forests, and rocky highlands. These lands brought long winters, dangerous seas, and difficult roads. They also offered deep harbors, rich deposits of ore, valuable timber, and natural defenses.

    An invading army can lose thousands of soldiers before reaching a city wall. A narrow valley forces an enemy into a predictable route. A mountain range shields a kingdom while filling its mines with wealth.

    Dark Elves learned to make hardship useful. They became skilled at taking resources from places other peoples considered too dangerous or unpleasant to settle.

    A High Elf searches for a place where mana gathers naturally. A Dark Elf looks at a storm-beaten coast beneath the mountains and asks:

    Can any of you truly hold a candle to where we live?

    The Matriarchy

    Dark Elf society has always been matriarchal. Great Houses revolve around women, and family authority passes through the female line. The matriarch and the women below her hold the most important positions in the household.

    A Dark Elf man becomes a celebrated warrior, commander, scholar, Praedari, or adviser. He holds considerable personal power. The structure of his House stays unchanged

    The Great Houses

    A powerful Dark Elf household is a dangerous place to grow up. Succession is never a quiet subject. Siblings become rivals before they are old enough to hold authority, and a marriage serves as a political weapon.

    Assassination is woven into Great House politics. Dark Elves consider killing an enemy in their sleep .Poison works . As normal, just another murder supposed to happen. A lie is more useful than a sword. Trust simply means that someone has missed the danger.

    Blood relation offers little protection when ambition is involved. A daughter arranges her mother’s murder for an inheritance. A sister will ruin another sister's reputation; then poison her. A husband will discover his marriage was arranged so his wife's House could gain access to something it wanted.

    A matriarch will learn that one of her daughters tried to kill her. If the attempt was clumsy, she will punish her. If it was clever, she will take a greater interest in her daughter's future.That interest will still involve torture.

    Cruelty

    Cruelty defines Dark Elf society. Slavery, ritual sacrifice, mutilation, and the deliberate torture of defeated enemies are all accepted parts of it.

    A noble will have a servant tortured for speaking out of turn. A commander will torment prisoners long after the battle is over. A household will punish an entire family for the failure of one member.

    Fear is useful. Sometimes suffering is the purpose.

    Public executions will become entertainment. Captives will be displayed as trophies. Punishments will be deliberately excessive, and a rival will be kept alive because death would end the satisfaction too quickly.

    Being captured by Dark Elves is among the worst fates their neighbors can imagine. Their enemies have good reason to fear it.

    Mana and the Blade

    Dark Elves hold one of the continent's most ancient martial mana traditions. They practice every major discipline, just as other elven civilizations do. Evocation and Necromancy stand as their strongest traditions.

    Evocation is prized for force that can overwhelm an opponent fast. Dark Elf Praedari are famed for destructive magic: power shaped to end a fight, cripple an enemy, or seize an advantage.

    Necromancy grew alongside it. For centuries, Dark Elf scholars have studied mana, life, death, vitality, and decay. Their work spans practical uses and practices that other civilizations view with disgust and fear.

    Dark Elves also forge some of the continent's finest enchanted melee weapons. Their swords, spears, daggers, and polearms are built to endure repeated impacts, heavy mana reinforcement, hostile magic, and prolonged use.

    A Dark Elf blade is expected to survive the violence it was made to deliver.

    Valerian artificers study these weapons. Imperial nobles collect them. Imperial Praedari wield them. Dark Elves know all of this.

    They will sell excellent weapons. They are far less willing to sell the knowledge behind their finest work.

    The Empire

    Dark Elves are openly hostile toward Valeria, yet they consistently speak with the Empire. They recognize what it is: a vast power that conquered weaker states, destroyed enemies that refused submission, and built institutions capable of holding what it took.

    Hatred does not stop recognition.

    Dark Elf rulers negotiate with Valeria when negotiation serves their interests. They discuss borders, arrange exchanges, bargain over prisoners, consider treaties. To them, speaking with a dangerous enemy is not submission.

    A treaty does not make them friends. It means both sides have decided that, for now, talking is useful.
