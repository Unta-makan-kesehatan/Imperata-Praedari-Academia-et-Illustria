    High Elves¶

    The Work Must Be Perfected¶

    High Elven cities were built around mana-rich places: valleys, islands, mountains, and coastlines where ambient mana gathered strongly. Once a site was chosen, roads, fields, and fortifications followed. If the land was difficult, High Elven skill was expected to answer the difficulty.

    For centuries, their confidence rested on real achievement. High Elven cities, crafts, and magical traditions were among the continent’s most sophisticated. Their long lives let masters refine a discipline over decades; their scholars inherited knowledge gathered over generations.

    A gift, in High Elven tradition, should not be left unfinished.

    A spell can be made more precise. A shield can be strengthened. A theory can be corrected. Even an ancient building may still have a flaw worth improving.

    The Cost of Doing It Their Way¶

    High Elves have traditionally been reluctant to adopt discoveries made elsewhere. It is not that they cannot understand foreign work. They would rather discover the answer themselves and then make it better.

    A High Elven scholar may spend decades solving a problem another people have already solved. A craftsman shown a superior dwarven method may try to devise a better High Elven one.

    This habit once cost them little. They were often ahead. But pride in their own knowledge can make it hard to recognize when another people has found a worthwhile path.

    Mana Made to Protect and Refine¶

    High Elves practice every major discipline of mana, with particular strengths in Abjuration and Transmutation. Their wards and barriers are valued for precision and stability. Their transmuters refine materials and alter physical properties with careful control.

    Their enchanters are especially renowned for armor and shields, combining protective magic with transformed and strengthened materials.

    High Elven pride is not empty. It is built on a history of mastery and can become a reason to resist learning.
